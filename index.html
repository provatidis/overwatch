    Overtime is an onchain sportsbook. Since July 2026 its treasury takes the other side of every bet, so the treasury is the house, and part of what the house wins buys OVER on the market and burns it. This page checks every day whether that keeps working.

    Thesis checks.

    Five things that have to hold for OVER’s buyback case to keep working. Each turns amber or red when its number crosses a limit.

      A daily job opens a GitHub issue in this project’s repo when a check gets worse, and closes it when the check recovers. The limits live in lib/config.js. A check whose data failed to refresh is marked and never alerts.

      Where the money went.

      What the house made, minus buybacks, should show up in its money: the treasury wallets, every betting pool and the house’s share of open bets, the casino and Speed Markets, with ETH and BTC counted at today’s prices. Whatever doesn’t is “other flows”: running costs, DEX incentives and swaps into other tokens.

      Supply and float.

      Read straight from the OVER contracts on Ethereum, Optimism, Arbitrum and Base. Burned counts every burn, including tokens burned from bets placed in OVER. The house’s OVER is what sits in the treasury wallets, Overtime’s admin multisigs, the OVER betting pools (the treasury is their only liquidity provider) and the casino’s and Speed Markets’ bankrolls. Free float is everything else.
